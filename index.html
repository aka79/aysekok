How can society develop the technical evidence, institutional capacity, and governance infrastructure needed to evaluate and responsibly deploy increasingly capable AI systems?

Research Overview

Ayse Kok Arslan researches how advanced AI systems can become more understandable, evaluable, governable, and accountable as they move from research environments into real-world institutions.

She works across the intersection of AI evaluation, assurance, frontier-model behavior, documentation, standards, and governance. A central focus of my work is connecting what we can observe and measure in AI systems—including capabilities, behavioral patterns, failure modes, robustness, and reliability—to the institutional processes needed to assess and govern those systems responsibly.

Her work draws on emerging approaches to AI evaluation and assurance, NIST and IEEE frameworks, international AI governance, and public-interest technology, with an emphasis on translating technical evidence into practical governance and institutional decision-making.

Pillars of Inquiry

🧪 AI Evaluation & Assurance

Evaluating advanced AI systems through behavioral testing, capability assessment, robustness, reliability, interpretability, and failure-mode analysis.

🧠 Frontier AI Behavior

Studying how increasingly capable models behave under different conditions, including agency, tool use, oversight, and alignment mechanisms.

🏛️ Governance & Standards

Exploring how technical evidence can inform governance, standards, assurance processes, and institutional design.

📚 Documentation & Accountability

Examining documentation, provenance, evaluation records, audit trails, and other forms of evidence as infrastructure for accountable AI.

🌐 Human-Centered AI

Studying the institutional and societal implications of AI deployment, with attention to human agency, education, trustworthy systems, and public-interest applications.
